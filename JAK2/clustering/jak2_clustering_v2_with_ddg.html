This is the complete feature set. The three dual-state ΔΔG features — ΔΔG in the closed state, in the open state, and their difference — were computed with PyRosetta on 4 November 2026 over all 21,508 substitutions, on two independent model pairs (43,016 ΔΔG pairs, 100% coverage). The features here come from pair 1: both states from one untemplated run, so nothing differs between the two structures except the conformation.

They change almost nothing. Against v1, which had eight features, this clustering agrees on 99.11% of variants (ARI 0.9644, 192 of 21,508 move) and the gnomAD odds ratio is unchanged at 1.97. Which pair is used matters even less: clustering on pair 2's ΔΔG instead moves 21 variants.

And ddg_delta should not be used at all — see section 3. It scores AUC 0.499, exact chance, and more importantly its values do not reproduce between the two model pairs (r = 0.074), so it is not measuring a property of the protein.

1. Method and seeds

Gower distance, which compares each pair of variants only on the features both have, so nothing is imputed. Semi-supervised K-means with 10 seed variants pinned to their clusters. Converged in 10 iterations.

The paper names 10 JAK1 seeds. JAK2 has no published equivalent, so they were derived by an explicit rule from data no feature saw:

  • C1 tolerant — the 5 ClinVar benign/likely-benign variants with the highest gnomAD allele frequency, all 2-star: R1063H, D1096E, C480F, N337D, K244R. Mirrors the paper's "common in the general population (from gnomAD v4)".
  • C2 damaging — the 5 ClinVar pathogenic/likely-pathogenic variants by review stars: V617F, R867Q, R938Q, P1095L, K539L. Mirrors "clinically reported to cause gain-of-function".
The seeds barely matter. Fifteen random 5+5 draws reproduce 0.9996–1.0000 of the labels. As on JAK1, the supervision orients the result — which pole is called "Cluster 1" — rather than determining its structure. So the hand-made seed rule above is not load bearing.

3. The dual-state ΔΔG features, and why one of them is unusable

PyRosetta, ΔΔG = ΔGWT − ΔGmut, so negative means destabilising — the opposite of FoldX. 21,508 substitutions on each of two model pairs. Pair 1 is untemplated (both states from one run); pair 2 is templated and exists purely as a check on whether these numbers describe the protein or the structures.

featureAUC pair 1 ↔ pair 2, Pearson rSpearman ρ verdict
ΔΔG closed0.7130.8930.928 reproducible
ΔΔG open0.7170.8700.906 reproducible
ΔΔG delta0.4990.074 0.155do not use

Two independent facts, and together they settle it. An AUC of 0.499 is exact chance — and on JAK1 the same feature scored 0.4966, so two proteins and two independent model sets agree. But the reproducibility column is the stronger evidence, and it is what the second model pair was computed for: ddg_closed and ddg_open survive a change of structures (r ≈ 0.88), so the ΔΔG calculation itself is stable. Their difference does not.

The cause is visible in the data: the median delta is −0.003. ΔΔG is local, and the two states differ only in where JH1 sits, so for almost every position nothing about the residue's environment changes between them. delta is therefore a difference of two large, strongly correlated numbers — dominated by numerical noise, and noise is exactly what fails to reproduce when the structures change.

So ddg_delta is not a weak feature. It is not measuring a property of the protein, and it is kept in this report only to document that.

Note also that ddg_closed and ddg_open correlate with each other at 0.896 — the highest pair in the whole matrix (section 4). They are effectively one feature, a general destabilisation term, which FoldX (AUC 0.742) already supplies at 94.6% coverage.

What this does not say. It does not say the closed model is wrong — the two closed structures agree to 1.53 Å and their ΔΔG values correlate at 0.893. And it does not rule out a conformational signal in principle: a difference restricted to the residues whose environment actually changes between states, the JH1–JH2 and JH1–FERM interfaces, might carry signal that a protein-wide difference drowns. That is a different feature from the one the paper specifies, and it has not been tested.

4. How the features relate to each other

Every feature is oriented before correlating — ESM-1b, SIFT and BLOSUM62 are multiplied by −1 — so that positive always means "agrees that this is damaging". Without that step the matrix shows large negative correlations that are an artefact of sign convention, not disagreement. After orienting, the lowest correlation in the whole matrix is -0.14: only ddg_delta fails to agree in direction with the rest.

Mean |ρ| across the 28 off-diagonal pairs is 0.434 — these features are not independent evidence. The tightest pairs are ddG closed/ddG open (0.90), CADD/ClinPred (0.84), ESM-1b/AlphaMissense (0.80); the loosest are ddG closed/ddG delta (-0.14), ESM-1b/ddG delta (-0.03), AlphaMissense/ddG delta (-0.01).

Two readings matter for the clustering:

  • The redundancy is concentrated. CADD, ClinPred, PolyPhen-2 and SIFT form a block that correlates at 0.75–0.84, which is unsurprising: ClinPred is a meta-predictor trained partly on the others, and all four lean heavily on conservation. Giving each of them weight 1, as the paper's method does, effectively weights that one signal four times. This is the strongest argument for the feature weights the paper does not publish.
  • BLOSUM62 and FoldX are the genuinely independent columns. BLOSUM62 correlates with everything else at 0.24–0.41 and FoldX at 0.39–0.50, against 0.64–0.84 among the rest. They are also the two weakest discriminators in section 2 (AUC 0.667 and 0.740). That is not a contradiction — they contribute information the others lack, which is exactly what a distance over mixed features should use them for, even though neither would be a good classifier alone.

The right-hand panel is the caveat. Pairwise coverage ranges from 6,335 variants — a dbNSFP feature against FoldX, the two incomplete sources intersected — through 6,697 for any two dbNSFP features, up to the full 21,508 among ESM-1b, AlphaMissense, PolyPhen-2 and BLOSUM62. The correlations in the dbNSFP block are therefore computed on a different, smaller and non-random subset than the ones among the complete features — the single-nucleotide-reachable 31.1% — so they are not strictly comparable to each other. The same asymmetry is what makes the gnomAD stratification in section 6 necessary.

Read this against the clustering, not instead of it. The clustering used these same eleven features, so a variant with every cell highlighted landing in the damaging cluster is not independent confirmation — it is the same evidence twice. What the table is good for is the disagreements: a row whose highlighting and whose cluster point opposite ways is where the per-feature thresholds and the multivariate distance part company, and those are the rows worth looking at by hand. Here, 3 variants carry 3 or more flags yet sit in the tolerant cluster: L805I (4/6), H345L (3/6), E846D (3/6); and no damaging-cluster variant carries fewer than three flags. The asymmetry is the point: a handful of flags is not enough to move a variant across the Gower boundary, because the clustering weighs how far past a threshold a value sits, not merely that it is past one. L805I is the clearest case — four of six predictors flag it, but none strongly, and it is observed in gnomAD.

Three caveats specific to this panel. S755R is the corrected form of a variant listed elsewhere as R755S — position 755 in O60674 is already S, so only S755R exists; all 27 missense entries here were checked residue by residue against the canonical sequence. R426X is nonsense and has no row: the universe is missense only, and no predictor in this set scores a stop-gain. And the last two groups overlap on four variants (G571R, V578I, L805I, V1110E), which are listed in both, as supplied.

6. gnomAD — the external test

gnomAD r4 holds 1,498 of these substitutions as observed human variation. No feature saw it.

The damaging cluster is depleted of observed variation — C1 9.87% against C2 5.27%, Fisher p = 4.3e-36. But the pooled odds ratio of 1.97 is confounded, exactly as on JAK1: 100.0% of gnomAD-matched variants fall in the dbNSFP-reachable subset, which is only 31.1% of all variants, and the tolerant cluster is disproportionately inside it (37.1% against 27.7%). Stratifying gives Mantel–Haenszel OR 1.54, which is the figure to quote.

7. ClinVar — the spot check

ClinVar has 347 JAK2 missense records but 305 are "Uncertain significance", leaving 21 usable labels. Too few for an odds ratio; enough to catch a clustering that has gone wrong.

Pathogenic (7/7 in the damaging cluster):
K539L → C2K607N → C2V617F → C2V617I → C2R867Q → C2R938Q → C2P1095L → C2

Benign (13/14 in the tolerant cluster):
T7M → C1G13V → C1R122H → C1G127D → C1K244R → C1N337D → C1L383V → C1V392M → C1C480F → C1N490S → C1L892V → C1Y1021F → C2R1063H → C1D1096E → C1

20 of 21 placed correctly, and critically V617F is in the damaging cluster — the canonical JAK2 gain-of-function mutation, and the one result that would have invalidated the whole exercise had it come out the other way. Note that 10 of these 21 were seeds, so the honest score on unseen labels is 10 of 11.

10. What this is, and what it is not

  • Not a reproduction. There is no published JAK2 clustering. Nothing here is an ARI against anyone.
  • All eleven features, but the conformational axis is inert — see section 3.
  • FoldX stands in for "MutFunc", as on JAK1, and is 94.6% rather than complete — the missing positions are 1–38, which ProtVar trims as AlphaFold's low-confidence N-terminus.
  • PolyPhen-2 HumVar was used. The paper does not name a model, and HumDiv disagrees on 23.4% of JAK2 variants. That choice is unexamined here and mattered more than any other on JAK1.
  • Equal feature weights — the paper's are unpublished. With ddg_closed and ddg_open correlating at 0.896 they are effectively one feature given double weight here.
  • ΔΔG from a single conformer per state, not an ensemble, and the interface residues where a conformational difference could be real sit in the least confident part of the model (AlphaFold FERM–JH1 PAE 21.0 Å against a 31 Å ceiling).
  • The gnomAD test is not mutability-controlled. CpG sites mutate ~10× faster.
