Why "wo_ddg_oc". Three of the paper's eleven features are ΔΔG in the closed state, in the open state, and their difference. Those need a dual-state Rosetta run on JAK2, which does not exist — so this version has eight features and no conformational axis. On JAK1 those three were the weakest of the set (ddg_delta AUC 0.4966, and dropping all three improved agreement with the paper, ARI 0.484 → 0.492), so the loss is real but probably small. A v2 with them is the obvious next step.

1. Method and seeds

Gower distance, which compares each pair of variants only on the features both have, so nothing is imputed. Semi-supervised K-means with 10 seed variants pinned to their clusters. Converged in 13 iterations.

The paper names 10 JAK1 seeds. JAK2 has no published equivalent, so they were derived by an explicit rule from data no feature saw:

  • C1 tolerant — the 5 ClinVar benign/likely-benign variants with the highest gnomAD allele frequency, all 2-star: R1063H, D1096E, C480F, N337D, K244R. Mirrors the paper's "common in the general population (from gnomAD v4)".
  • C2 damaging — the 5 ClinVar pathogenic/likely-pathogenic variants by review stars: V617F, R867Q, R938Q, P1095L, K539L. Mirrors "clinically reported to cause gain-of-function".
The seeds barely matter. Fifteen random 5+5 draws reproduce 0.9998–1.0000 of the labels. As on JAK1, the supervision orients the result — which pole is called "Cluster 1" — rather than determining its structure. So the hand-made seed rule above is not load bearing.

3. How the features relate to each other

Every feature is oriented before correlating — ESM-1b, SIFT and BLOSUM62 are multiplied by −1 — so that positive always means "agrees that this is damaging". Without that step the matrix shows large negative correlations that are an artefact of sign convention, not disagreement. After orienting, the lowest correlation in the whole matrix is +0.24: no pair of these eight features disagrees in direction at all.

Mean |ρ| across the 28 off-diagonal pairs is 0.577 — these features are not independent evidence. The tightest pairs are CADD/ClinPred (0.84), ESM-1b/AlphaMissense (0.80), PolyPhen-2/ClinPred (0.79); the loosest are FoldX/BLOSUM62 (0.24), PolyPhen-2/BLOSUM62 (0.26), ESM-1b/BLOSUM62 (0.35).

Two readings matter for the clustering:

  • The redundancy is concentrated. CADD, ClinPred, PolyPhen-2 and SIFT form a block that correlates at 0.75–0.84, which is unsurprising: ClinPred is a meta-predictor trained partly on the others, and all four lean heavily on conservation. Giving each of them weight 1, as the paper's method does, effectively weights that one signal four times. This is the strongest argument for the feature weights the paper does not publish.
  • BLOSUM62 and FoldX are the genuinely independent columns. BLOSUM62 correlates with everything else at 0.24–0.41 and FoldX at 0.39–0.50, against 0.64–0.84 among the rest. They are also the two weakest discriminators in section 2 (AUC 0.667 and 0.740). That is not a contradiction — they contribute information the others lack, which is exactly what a distance over mixed features should use them for, even though neither would be a good classifier alone.

The right-hand panel is the caveat. Pairwise coverage ranges from 6,335 variants — a dbNSFP feature against FoldX, the two incomplete sources intersected — through 6,697 for any two dbNSFP features, up to the full 21,508 among ESM-1b, AlphaMissense, PolyPhen-2 and BLOSUM62. The correlations in the dbNSFP block are therefore computed on a different, smaller and non-random subset than the ones among the complete features — the single-nucleotide-reachable 31.1% — so they are not strictly comparable to each other. The same asymmetry is what makes the gnomAD stratification in section 5 necessary.

Read this against the clustering, not instead of it. The clustering used these same eight features, so a variant with every cell highlighted landing in the damaging cluster is not independent confirmation — it is the same evidence twice. What the table is good for is the disagreements: a row whose highlighting and whose cluster point opposite ways is where the per-feature thresholds and the multivariate distance part company, and those are the rows worth looking at by hand. Here, 3 variants carry 3 or more flags yet sit in the tolerant cluster: L805I (4/6), H345L (3/6), E846D (3/6); and no damaging-cluster variant carries fewer than three flags. The asymmetry is the point: a handful of flags is not enough to move a variant across the Gower boundary, because the clustering weighs how far past a threshold a value sits, not merely that it is past one. L805I is the clearest case — four of six predictors flag it, but none strongly, and it is observed in gnomAD.

Three caveats specific to this panel. S755R is the corrected form of a variant listed elsewhere as R755S — position 755 in O60674 is already S, so only S755R exists; all 27 missense entries here were checked residue by residue against the canonical sequence. R426X is nonsense and has no row: the universe is missense only, and no predictor in this set scores a stop-gain. And the last two groups overlap on four variants (G571R, V578I, L805I, V1110E), which are listed in both, as supplied.

5. gnomAD — the external test

gnomAD r4 holds 1,498 of these substitutions as observed human variation. No feature saw it.

The damaging cluster is depleted of observed variation — C1 9.91% against C2 5.3%, Fisher p = 8.5e-36. But the pooled odds ratio of 1.96 is confounded, exactly as on JAK1: 100.0% of gnomAD-matched variants fall in the dbNSFP-reachable subset, which is only 31.1% of all variants, and the tolerant cluster is disproportionately inside it (37.3% against 27.6%). Stratifying gives Mantel–Haenszel OR 1.52, which is the figure to quote.

6. ClinVar — the spot check

ClinVar has 347 JAK2 missense records but 305 are "Uncertain significance", leaving 21 usable labels. Too few for an odds ratio; enough to catch a clustering that has gone wrong.

19 of 21 placed correctly, and critically V617F is in the damaging cluster — the canonical JAK2 gain-of-function mutation, and the one result that would have invalidated the whole exercise had it come out the other way. Note that 10 of these 21 are seeds — they were chosen from ClinVar — so the honest score on unseen labels is 9 of 11.

9. What this is, and what it is not

  • Not a reproduction. There is no published JAK2 clustering. Nothing here is an ARI against anyone.
  • Eight features, not eleven. No conformational axis at all.
  • FoldX stands in for "MutFunc", as on JAK1, and is 94.6% rather than complete — the missing positions are 1–38, which ProtVar trims as AlphaFold's low-confidence N-terminus.
  • PolyPhen-2 HumVar was used. The paper does not name a model, and HumDiv disagrees on 23.4% of JAK2 variants. That choice is unexamined here and mattered more than any other on JAK1.
  • Equal feature weights — the paper's are unpublished.
  • The gnomAD test is not mutability-controlled. CpG sites mutate ~10× faster.
