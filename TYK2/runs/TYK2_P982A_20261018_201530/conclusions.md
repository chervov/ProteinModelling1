## Power test result: the JH2-JH1 readout does not survive a third batch

Pooling all 31 P982A seeds at 32/64 against the 15 wild-type seeds:

| JH2-JH1 (A) | n | median | vs wild type (MW) |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| wild type | 15 | 49.3 | - |
| P982A seeds 0-15 | 15 | **53.5** | p = 0.097 (looser) |
| P982A seeds 16-31 | 16 | **45.0** | p = 0.093 (tighter) |
| **P982A pooled** | **31** | **46.5** | **p = 0.96 (nothing)** |

**The two P982A batches differ from each other at p = 0.0005** (53.5 vs 45.0 A) and straddle wild type, each
trending to near-significance in the *opposite* direction. Pooled, the effect is exactly nil.

**This retracts the proposal to use JH2-JH1 as the TYK2 readout.** The earlier p = 0.097 was one batch of a
coordinate whose batch-to-batch spread (8.5 A between medians) is larger than any difference it was being
used to detect.

It also downgrades the positive control. V678F's JH2-JH1 result (pooled p = 0.028 vs wild type) rests on
16 seeds whose own two halves differ by 4.3 A (56.1 vs 51.8, p = 0.066) - the same instability, just below
the threshold where it was noticed. A 5.7 A separation from wild type cannot be trusted when the same
condition moves 8.5 A between batches.

### The underlying tension

At 32/64 the two coordinates fail in opposite ways:

| coordinate | P982A batch 1 vs 2 | informative? |
|---|---|---|
| FERM-JH1 | 42.4 vs 42.9 A, p = 0.57 (stable) | no - 0/31 open, saturated at the floor |
| JH2-JH1 | 53.5 vs 45.0 A, p = 0.0005 (unstable) | no - noise exceeds the effect |

**A coordinate in this setup is either saturated and reproducible, or responsive and irreproducible.**
That is the central obstacle to measuring TYK2 mutation effects here, and no depth tested so far escapes it.

FERM-JH1 for P982A pooled over 31 seeds: median 42.5 vs wild type 45.2 A, 0/31 open vs 0/15, p = 0.14 -
still no effect, now on twice the seeds.

### What would actually help

1. **Quantify the batch effect before interpreting any more comparisons.** Three or four wild-type batches
   of 8 seeds at one depth would give the between-batch variance directly, and every mutation effect could
   then be judged against it rather than against within-batch scatter.
2. **Find the source.** The runs differ in sandbox, GPU and MSA rebuild. An MSA cached once and reused
   across batches would separate "MSA sampling" from "everything else" - the MSA is rebuilt per run today,
   so it is the prime suspect.
3. Only then return to mutation screening.
