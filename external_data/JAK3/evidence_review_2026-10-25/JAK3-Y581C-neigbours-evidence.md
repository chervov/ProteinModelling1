# JAK3 Y581C: evidence about neighbouring residues

Research date: 25 October 2026. Scientific evidence summary, not a clinical classification.

**The sequence neighbourhood of JAK3 Y581 contains both activating and impaired-function mutations. It supports investigating Y581C, but cannot establish whether Y581C is gain-of-function (GOF), loss-of-function (LOF), or functionally neutral.**

These are neighbours along the amino-acid sequence in the regulatory JH2 pseudokinase domain. Sequence distance is not a measurement of three-dimensional proximity.

## Nearby variants

| Variant | Position relative to Y581 | Evidence and interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| **A572V** | −9 | Experimentally activating: supported factor-independent Ba/F3 growth and produced features of megakaryoblastic leukemia in a mouse model. Walters et al., 2006, PMID 16843266. |
| **R582W** | **+1** | Reported in severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID). A primary patient series lists it with another JAK3 variant, Q39fsX51. This is an impaired-function disease context, not evidence of activation or a direct result for Y581C. |
| **H583Y** | **+2** | Experimentally activating: found in NK/T-cell lymphoma and supported IL-3-independent Ba/F3 growth. Sim et al., 2017, PMID 28284718. |
| **H588P** | +7 | Reported in a SCID patient cohort. That observation alone does not establish its isolated biochemical effect. |
| **G589D** | +8 | Experimentally activating in the Sim et al. lymphoma study, including IL-3-independent Ba/F3 growth. |
| **G589S** | +8 | Experimentally impaired function: abolished kinase activity while retaining association with the receptor common gamma chain. Studied in JAK3-deficient SCID by Roberts et al., 2004, PMID 14615376. |

## The closest activating precedent and drug measurements

**H583Y is only two residues from Y581.** In the Sim et al. experiments, tofacitinib inhibited growth driven by H583Y with a cellular IC50 of **85 ± 10 nM**, and growth driven by G589D with an IC50 of **54 ± 9 nM**. These are laboratory measurements for those substitutions, not Y581C drug-sensitivity measurements or clinical response estimates. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajpath.2017.01.004).

## Why the neighbours cannot determine Y581C's direction

**G589D versus G589S is the clearest caution:** different substitutions at the same residue produced activating versus impaired-function outcomes in the reported experimental settings. The amino-acid replacement and biological context matter, not simply the position.

The immediately adjacent R582W has immunodeficiency-associated evidence, whereas H583Y two positions away is experimentally activating. Thus this neighbourhood is functionally important but does not uniformly act as an activation hotspot for every possible substitution.

For Y581C, the neighbouring variants support a hypothesis of altered regulation. They do not resolve whether Y581C increases signaling, reduces signaling, or has little effect. No verified exact-Y581C functional or drug-response experiment was established in this review.

## R582W annotation caveats

The [ClinVar R582W record, variation 36415](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar/variation/36415/) collects SCID observations and notes a possible splicing effect in a submitter's assessment. Therefore, its evidence should not be reduced automatically to a pure amino-acid-level mechanism. The record also notes unreliable population-frequency metrics at this position. Neither issue establishes anything about Y581C.
