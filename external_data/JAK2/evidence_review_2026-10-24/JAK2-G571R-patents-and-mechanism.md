# JAK2 G571R: patent lineage, mechanistic evidence and drug claims

Search updated 25 October 2026. Follow-up to the four-variant review. This is a scientific evidence audit, not a patent-validity opinion or a clinical variant classification.

The exact JAK2 G571R variant has a published patient-sample observation and a plausible regulatory hypothesis. I did not locate an independent experiment directly establishing G571R activation, diminished Tyr570 phosphorylation, or a G571R-specific drug response. Independent experiments do support inhibitory Tyr570 biology. Those two statements must remain separate.

## Original observation

[Ma et al., 2009, Mutation Profile of JAK2 Transcripts in Patients with Chronic Myeloproliferative Neoplasias](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2607565/), PMID 19074595, DOI 10.2353/jmoldx.2009.080114, reported one heterozygous G571R observation in approximately 20,000 samples submitted for suspected MPN. G571S was separately reported in three cases. A suspected-MPN referral cohort is not the general population and does not establish population prevalence.

The authors proposed that changing Gly571 could interfere with phosphorylation of adjacent Tyr570 and thereby remove negative regulation. They did not establish that sequence of events experimentally for G571R. Their reference for inhibitory Tyr570 was Feener et al. 2004.

## The diagnostic patent family

Title: **Compositions and methods for detecting mutations in JAK2 nucleic acid**. Inventors: **Wanlong Ma and Maher Albitar**. Applicant/assignee lineage: **Quest Diagnostics Investments**. The family claims priority to 31 October 2008; therefore, describing it simply as subsequent to the 2009 paper was imprecise. Its public patent documents appeared later, but the priority claim predates the paper's journal issue.

These documents are related filings, continuations or divisionals; they are not separate biological replications. The same investigators and matching one-case table support treating their G571R observation as overlapping evidence, not an additional patient cohort.

**What is actually disclosed:** Table 1 records G571R, one case, heterozygous. Table 2 records `g2205c`, codon `ggt>cgt`, Gly→Arg. This is the patent's sequence-relative notation; do not substitute it for transcript-based HGVS `c.1711G>C` without accounting for the reference sequence. The disclosure concerns mutation detection using nucleic-acid/protein methods. It supplies no G571R-specific kinase assay, Tyr570 phosphorylation comparison, transforming assay or inhibitor dose-response result that I could identify. Some family documents discuss experiments on other JAK2 alterations; those do not validate G571R.

Mention in a patent description, inclusion in a dependent claim, and experimental demonstration are different categories. A granted patent does not by itself establish variant pathogenicity or therapeutic sensitivity.

## A separate treatment patent that also mentions G571R

[WO2016168612A1, Methods for treating myeloproliferative disorders](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2016168612A1/en), published 20 October 2016, priority 15 April 2015. Inventors Elizabeth Trehu and Richard M. Jack; original assignee Promedior Inc.

It concerns serum amyloid P/pentraxin-2, including recombinant **PRM-151**, in myeloproliferative disorders. Its list of example JAK2 mutations includes G571R and explicitly cites **US2010/0112571**. I found no outcome attributed specifically to a G571R carrier and no G571R-selective drug assay. Thus, this is a real treatment-related mention, but not evidence that PRM-151 treats G571R-driven disease. Related publications include US20180318303A1, EP3283655A1 and JP2018512164A.

Another nearby search result, [WO2014068072A1, Identification, assessment and therapy of essential thrombocythemia with resistance to JAK2 inhibitors](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2014068072A1/en), contains functional/drug experiments involving **R867Q and S755R/R938Q**, including JAK2 and HSP90 inhibitors. Those experiments do not concern G571R. Its drug-resistance title must not be imported into the G571R annotation.

## Independent experiments supporting Tyr570 biology

1. **Feener et al., 2004**, [Tyrosine phosphorylation of Jak2 in the JH2 domain inhibits cytokine signaling](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15143188/), DOI 10.1128/MCB.24.11.4968-4978.2004. Mass spectrometry identified phosphorylation at Tyr570. Y570F increased basal signaling and prolonged cytokine-induced activation. The inhibitory effect was separable from SOCS3. This is the experimental foundation cited by Ma, but Y570F is not G571R.

2. **Argetsinger et al., 2004**, [Autophosphorylation of JAK2 on Tyrosines 221 and 570 Regulates Its Activity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC416404/), PMID 15143187, DOI 10.1128/MCB.24.11.4955-4967.2004. Independent site mapping and mutagenesis supported an inhibitory role for Tyr570. Again, this tests the phosphorylation site, not the adjacent arginine substitution.

3. **Ungureanu et al., 2011**, [The pseudokinase domain of JAK2 is a dual-specificity protein kinase that negatively regulates cytokine signaling](https://www.nature.com/articles/nsmb.2099), DOI 10.1038/nsmb.2099. Demonstrated JH2 phosphorylation of Ser523 and Tyr570 and increased basal signaling when JH2 catalytic activity was disabled. Reduced Tyr570 phosphorylation was also observed in V617F patient cells. This supplies a mechanistic framework, not exact-G571R validation.

4. **Bandaranayake et al., 2012**, [Crystal structures of the Jak2 pseudokinase domain and the pathogenic mutant V617F](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3414675/). Provides structural context and evidence for JH2 phosphorylation of Ser523 in cis and Tyr570 in trans. The structurally studied pathogenic mutant was V617F.

5. **Li et al., 2025**, [The PRL2 phosphatase up-regulates miR-21 through activation of the JAK2/STAT3 pathway to down-regulate the PTEN tumor suppressor](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39665584/), DOI 10.1042/BCJ20240626. Substrate-trapping and mass-spectrometry evidence connected PRL2 to JAK2 Tyr570 dephosphorylation; PRL2 deletion increased Tyr570 phosphorylation approximately eight-fold in the tested MEFs. The work links this regulation to STAT3/miR-21/PTEN signaling. It is a newer experimental lead for the regulatory site, not a study of G571R or a proven G571R therapy.

## G571S: useful evidence, but a different allele

[Milosevic Feenstra et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26423830/) tested G571S among noncanonical mutations; the MPL/TPO-related functional assays did not establish constitutive activation for G571S. See the [primary paper, Figure 4](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article-pdf/127/3/325/1393686/325.pdf).

[Lin et al., 2019, JAK2 p.G571S in B-cell precursor acute lymphoblastic leukemia: a synergizing germline susceptibility](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41375-019-0459-z), DOI 10.1038/s41375-019-0459-z, found that G571S did not support IL-3-independent growth in ordinary BaF3 cells, but did in BaF3 cells expressing CRLF2 and IL7R-alpha. STAT3 K370R provided an additional growth advantage and altered downstream signaling. This is direct, context-dependent functional evidence for **G571S**. The proposed effect on Tyr570 remains a mechanistic interpretation rather than a demonstrated G571R result.

Consequently, a blanket description of G571S as having no functional effect would be misleading. Equally, those results do not justify labelling G571R activating: glycine-to-serine and glycine-to-arginine have different chemical and structural consequences.

## What can be concluded about drugs and modelling?

No G571R-specific clinical response, resistance result, or mutant-versus-wild-type inhibitor IC50 was located in the accessible sources searched. The diagnostic patents, PRM-151 patent mention and kinase-hotspot catalogue do not fill that gap. This search does not establish absence from unpublished work, unindexed supplements or licensed databases.

For modelling, the strongest concrete question is whether G571R changes the local Tyr570 loop, accessibility or regulatory interactions. My inference is that receptor context and phosphorylation state should be considered explicitly. An unphosphorylated isolated-domain model cannot by itself establish a phosphorylation-dependent activation mechanism. Nor does a predicted conformational change establish inhibitor sensitivity.

An eventual experimental comparison would need to distinguish expression/stability effects, Tyr570 phosphorylation, downstream signaling and inhibitor response. An adjacent mutation can also affect phospho-antibody recognition, so apparent loss of a phospho-signal would need independent confirmation. These are research considerations, not evidence already demonstrated for G571R.

## Search scope and corrected wording

Queries used combinations of JAK2 with G571R, Gly571Arg, G571S, Tyr570/Y570, phosphorylation, functional, ruxolitinib, inhibitor and patent, plus exact paper titles. Sources included PubMed/PMC, journal pages, Google Patents, official EPO documents, patent-family metadata and the previously saved NLM database results. Related-patent and cited-reference chains were followed. Some full-text pages failed retrieval; indexed text and accessible primary-document versions were used where possible. No claim is made to have searched every proprietary database or every patent worldwide.

Suggested replacement for the original sentence:

> Ma et al. proposed that substitutions at Gly571 could disrupt inhibitory Tyr570 phosphorylation. The G571R observation is reproduced in the related Quest diagnostic patent family and catalogued by COSMIC; later reviews and a separate treatment patent repeat or cite this evidence. Independent studies validate Tyr570 regulation and demonstrate context-dependent effects of G571S, but direct functional and drug-response validation of G571R was not located.
