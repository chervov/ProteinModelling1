JAK1 variant clustering — exploratory analysis

All 21,926 substitutions from jak1_clustering_rebuilt.csv · paper's Dataset EV1 clustering vs the rebuild · gnomAD r4 · generated 3 November 2026

Two clusterings of the same 21,926 JAK1 missense substitutions — the one published in Dataset EV1 and the one rebuilt here — agree on 84.9% of variants, ARI 0.484. This report asks what the features look like, how well each separates the clusters, where the two disagree, and whether either agrees with population genetics.

Cluster identity. Nothing in Dataset EV1 states which label means what. From the data it is unambiguous: cluster 1 = tolerant (AlphaMissense median 0.166), cluster 2 = damaging (median 0.953). All of this report uses that convention.

1. Feature distributions

Eleven features, each shown separately for the paper's two clusters. Coverage is in the panel title: five features are complete, three sit at 31.3% because dbNSFP indexes only single-nucleotide changes, and FoldX is at 94.9%.

AlphaMissense, PolyPhen-2 and ClinPred are strongly bimodal and separate the clusters almost completely. The two state-specific ΔΔG features are shifted but heavily overlapping, and ddg_delta is all but identical between clusters.

Sign convention, easy to get wrong. The Rosetta columns are ΔΔG = ΔGWT − ΔGmut, so negative = destabilising — the opposite of FoldX in the same table, where positive = destabilising (build_rosetta_ddg_jak1_info.md §2). Everything in this report is oriented so that larger always means more damaging. Scored with the wrong sign, ΔΔG closed appears to have AUC 0.26; oriented correctly it is 0.74.

2. Feature correlation

Spearman ρ on pairwise-complete observations, every feature oriented so that larger means more damaging, so all genuine agreement appears positive.

BLOSUM62 is the outlier — it is the only position-blind feature, a 20×20 lookup, so every V→F scores the same wherever it sits. The ΔΔG features correlate strongly with each other and weakly with everything else, which is expected: they measure thermodynamic stability, while the rest measure evolutionary or clinical pathogenicity. These are different quantities.

On equal footing, AlphaMissense is the best feature — 0.9470 against ClinPred's 0.9463 — and PolyPhen-2 (0.9423) overtakes CADD (0.9284). The apparent lead of ClinPred and CADD in the previous table is an artefact of which variants they are scored on, not of how well they score them.

The dbNSFP subset is intrinsically easier. Every feature with full coverage gains on it: AlphaMissense +0.061, PolyPhen-2 +0.058, BLOSUM62 +0.022, FoldX +0.018. Single-nucleotide-reachable substitutions are the milder, better-characterised ones, so a clustering evaluated only there looks better than it is.

ESM-1b is the most robust. It is the only strong feature that barely moves between the two halves (0.8964 → 0.8884) and it is the best feature on the harder half, ahead of AlphaMissense (0.8551) and PolyPhen-2 (0.8515). Where the genomic predictors cannot go at all, the protein language model still works — which is the argument for weighting it more heavily than its all-variant AUC suggests.

4. gnomAD enrichment — the external validation

gnomAD r4 holds 984 missense variants for JAK1, 982 of which match a substitution in this dataset. Neither clustering used gnomAD, so the extent to which observed human variation avoids the damaging cluster is an independent test of both.

Both clusterings pass, and they pass equally. The paper's damaging cluster is 2.56× depleted of gnomAD-observed variants (7.46% vs 2.91%, Fisher p = 5.0e-51); the rebuild gives 2.54× (7.15% vs 2.82%, p = 1.2e-49). Purifying selection is recovered by an unsupervised split that never saw population data. Critically, the two clusterings are indistinguishable on this criterion despite disagreeing on one variant in seven — so ARI 0.484 does not mean one is right and the other wrong.

Along the protein

The two curves run opposite: Spearman ρ = -0.456 (p = 1.3e-03, 47 windows). The JH1 kinase domain is the most damaging-dense and the most gnomAD-depleted region of the protein; the FERM–SH2 linker region is the most tolerant.

The reproduction is substantially better than ARI 0.484 suggests. Where both clusterings have the data to decide — at least 10 of 11 features and an unambiguous AlphaMissense score — they agree 94.3% at ARI 0.786. The headline is dragged down by 4,976 variants with sparse features and an intermediate score, which disagree 68% of the time at ARI 0.130, barely above the 53.6% permutation null. Those are substitutions needing two or three nucleotide changes, so CADD, SIFT and ClinPred are absent by construction, leaving Gower distance over 7–8 dimensions on a variant that is ambiguous anyway. Neither label is trustworthy there.

This reorders the candidate explanations for the gap. Dropping all three ΔΔG features moves ARI 0.484 → 0.492; the HumDiv/HumVar choice is worth about ΔARI 0.090. Stratifying by data quality moves it 0.484 → 0.786 — an order of magnitude more explanatory power than either. The dominant cause is missingness interacting with the Gower distance, not a methodological difference between the two pipelines.

7. What follows

  • Both clusterings are externally valid and equally so. 2.5× gnomAD depletion each, p ≈ 10−50, from a split that never saw population data.
  • The published assignment carries very different per-variant confidence, and EV1 does not mark which. A variant in the well-determined set (n = 4,931) is solid; one in the ill-determined set (n = 4,976) is close to arbitrary. A confidence field — feature count plus distance to the decision boundary — would be more useful than further tuning of the clustering.
  • The ΔΔG features discriminate, but do not help. Oriented correctly, ΔΔG closed and open reach AUC 0.74 — on a par with FoldX at 0.72 — so they are not uninformative. Yet dropping all three improves agreement with the paper (ARI 0.484 → 0.492), which says they are largely redundant with FoldX while adding their own noise. ddg_delta is the genuine null at AUC 0.497.
  • Coverage is the lever worth pulling. The three 31.3% features are missing precisely where the clustering is least certain. Running SIFT and CADD natively, as PolyPhen-2 already was, would close most of the ill-determined set.

Methods

Source JAK1/clustering/jak1_clustering_rebuilt.csv (21,926 rows, 11 features, both cluster labels). gnomAD r4 missense variants fetched from the GraphQL API by gene symbol and matched on (position, alternate residue); 982 of 984 matched. AUC computed as the rank-sum statistic on each feature's own coverage, with features oriented so larger = more damaging. Enrichment tested by Fisher's exact test. ARI is the standard adjusted Rand index. Scripts: fetch_gnomad.py, analyse.py, plots.py, plots2.py, build_html.py.
