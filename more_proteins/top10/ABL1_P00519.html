How to read these numbers. Bit score is the alignment score on a log2 scale, independent of database size: it is the number to rank by. Above ~50 means real homology, above ~200 means unambiguous homology, and above ~1000 over near-full coverage means a close paralogue. E-value is how many hits this good would be expected by chance in these 20,431 sequences; 10−3 was the cutoff, and 0.0 means below floating-point resolution, not zero evidence. It depends on database size, so it is not comparable across searches of different databases. % identity is counted over aligned columns only, and % similarity adds conservative substitutions. Below about 25–30% identity — the “twilight zone” — identity alone stops discriminating homology from chance, which is exactly where the bit score and E-value still do. % query covered is aligned columns over the full query length: a high bit score with low coverage (ABL1 and BTK against the SRC-family kinases, ~39–66%) means one shared domain matched, not the whole protein — borrow a kinase domain from such a hit, never an inter-domain arrangement.

9. Literature review

9.1 Conformations: is there a real open and a real closed state?

What is actually known about this protein occupying more than one inter-domain arrangement.

  • ABL1 is held inactive by an SH3–SH2 clamp on the back of the kinase domain plus a myristoyl group bound in a C-lobe pocket
    1OPL is the autoinhibited assembly used here as the closed state.
  • Kincore records ABL1 as the most conformationally balanced kinase in the PDB — 44 DFGin / 88 DFGout / 58 DFGinter checked this session
    That intra-domain DFG axis is distinct from the inter-domain clamp measured here.

9.2 Mutations

Primary reports behind the substitutions computed in section 6.

  • T315I is the gatekeeper mutation, found in 2–20% of resistance cases. Imatinib, dasatinib and nilotinib all depend on a hydrogen bond to Thr315; the bulkier isoleucine removes it and adds steric hindrance — link checked this session
  • P-loop (Y253H, E255K) and activation-loop (H396R) substitutions are the other recurrent imatinib-resistance classes

9.3 Drugs and drug resistance

Why the conformational question is a therapeutic question for this protein.

  • Asciminib binds the myristoyl pocket rather than the ATP site and blocks T315I, overcoming resistance to ATP-competitive inhibitors — link checked this session
    The cleanest clinical demonstration that an allosteric site can bypass a gatekeeper mutation.
  • Ponatinib retains moderate activity against T315I at therapeutic concentrations — link checked this session

9.4 Associated disease

  • BCR-ABL1 fusion drives chronic myeloid leukaemia and Ph+ ALL checked this session

10. Caveats — read before using any number here

1. Some ΔΔG magnitudes are repulsion, not free energies. The protocol repacks only an 8 Å shell around the mutated site and applies no global FastRelax (that choice buys ~100k variants/hour instead of ~11 minutes per structure). When a larger side chain cannot be accommodated by local repacking alone, the Lennard-Jones repulsive term dominates and the result is an arbitrary large positive number. Any |ΔΔG| above 25 REU is flagged with ⚠ and should be read as “sterically impossible without backbone relaxation”, not as a measured energy. Affected here: T315I, F317L.
2. “Open” and “closed” here mean the inter-domain arrangement. The same two words are used in the kinase literature for at least three other things: the G-loop over the ATP site (a few Å), the DFG-in/DFG-out flip (~10 Å), and the WPD loop in phosphatases. A paper reporting that a mutation “stabilises the closed conformation” may well be talking about the active site, not the domain arrangement computed here. See open_close/open_close_inf.md in this repository for the measured comparison of the senses.
3. The predictor panel is blind to gain of function. Every member scores loss of function or loss of stability. A constitutively activating mutation is therefore expected to score benign, and does. Cluster membership is evidence about tolerance, never about direction of effect.
4. Renumbering is sequence-based. Template and experimental coordinates arrive in author numbering; each is mapped to canonical UniProt positions by the offset maximising sequence identity, and the achieved identity is reported in section 1. An offset with low identity means the mapping is unreliable and every per-residue number from that model with it.
5. Coverage limits the geometry. A centroid distance computed from a partial domain is not comparable with one from the complete domain. Pairs where either domain is under 70% present are marked with * in section 2.
