JAK2 selected variants — multi-method ΔΔG

24 variants supplied by the user (14 previously reported gain-of-function, 10 identified in this study), scored against UniProt O60674 on the AlphaFold model AF-O60674-F1. All methods signed so that positive = destabilising.

One-line summary. As a group these variants are less destabilising than a typical JAK2 substitution (median 0.34 vs background 0.74 kcal/mol, 33rd percentile), which is the expected signature of gain-of-function through release of JH2 autoinhibition rather than unfolding. The four predictors agree with each other only at r = 0.46–0.67, so individual values carry large uncertainty; seven variants are called destabilising above the noise floor by all four, but only I1065T clears the per-variant error bar.

3. Solvent accessibility (RSA) — and why it does not rescue the predictions here

RSA (relative solvent accessibility) is the solvent-exposed surface area of the wild-type residue in the folded structure, divided by the maximum that residue type could expose if fully extended and isolated, expressed as a percentage. It is measured from the AlphaFold model geometry, not predicted, and it depends only on the wild-type residue — not on what you mutate to. mCSM returns it alongside each prediction. 0% is fully buried in the core; above ~50% is surface.

It is in this table for two reasons. First, burial is normally the dominant physical determinant of substitution cost: a buried residue is packed against neighbours with no solvent to absorb a change, while a surface residue has room to accommodate almost anything. Second, RSA is a strong baseline in its own right — RSA alone reaches Spearman 0.480 on the ProteinGym stability slice, and RSA combined with conservation log-odds reaches 0.551, above ESM2-650M at 0.496 (10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf322). A method that cannot beat RSA is not earning its complexity.

The expected RSA–ΔΔG relationship is absent in this set. Spearman correlation between RSA and each method: ThermoMPNN −0.18 (p=0.41), FoldX −0.08 (p=0.72), mCSM −0.29 (p=0.17), DDMut −0.36 (p=0.10); mean of the four −0.20 (p=0.37). None reaches significance. The burial ordering is not even monotonic — the exposed group (+0.94) scores higher than the partially buried group (+0.48). With n=23 this is weak evidence either way, but it means RSA cannot be used here to adjudicate between methods that disagree.

Two readings are consistent with that. The benign one: 23 variants is too small, and the set is deliberately non-random — these are variants selected for a phenotype, not sampled from the protein. The substantive one: a gain-of-function set is expected to avoid the core, because unfolding the kinase would destroy the activity the phenotype depends on. The absence of a burial signal is then a property of the biology, not a failure of the measurement.

3.2 Where burial does corroborate a prediction

  • I1065T — RSA 3.8%, buried, and the largest ΔΔG in the set (+2.32 ThermoMPNN, +3.15 FoldX). Internally coherent, and the one variant that clears the ±1.47 error bar.
  • H608N — RSA 1.5%, the most deeply buried residue here, and all four methods agree it destabilises (+0.96 to +2.11). Physically consistent even though it does not clear the error bar.
  • T875N — RSA 79.8%, fully exposed, and uniformly near zero (−0.02 to +0.34). Coherent in the opposite direction.
  • G571R — RSA 73.9%, yet ThermoMPNN (+1.15) and FoldX (+2.44) call it substantially destabilising while mCSM (+0.35) and DDMut (−0.05) call it nothing. At that exposure an arginine has somewhere to go, so the low values are the more physically plausible pair — the one case in this set where RSA does arbitrate.
  • V617F — RSA 44.3%, partly exposed, consistent with its uniformly small ΔΔG and with an allosteric rather than structural mechanism.

4. RSALOR — a conservation-based fifth column

The four methods above are all physics- or structure-learned stability predictors. RSALOR (10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf322, Tsishyn, Hermans, Pucci & Rooman, ULB Brussels — the same group behind PoPMuSiC) is a different axis: an independent-site evolutionary log-odds ratio (LOR) of wild-type versus mutant residue frequency in homologues, scaled by relative solvent accessibility. Higher = more deleterious. It is unsupervised with no training set, so it cannot have memorised these variants.

Read this before using the LOR numbers. RSALOR needs a multiple sequence alignment, which it does not build. I assembled one from the UniRef50 clusters of all four JAK-family kinases (JAK2 O60674, JAK1 P23458, JAK3 P52333, TYK2 P29597), MAFFT-aligned: 902 sequences, 877 after redundancy removal. But the effective depth is only Neff = 30.2 — the clusters are dominated by vertebrate orthologues above 80% identity, which collapse into few independent observations. Adding the three paralogues raised Neff only from 23.9 to 30.2. Residue frequencies estimated from ~30 effective sequences are noisy, so these values are substantially weaker than the published benchmark, which used ProteinGym's deep alignments. A jackhmmer or HHblits search against UniRef90/BFD would fix it but needs a ~25 GB database not available here.

What it adds. RSALOR ranks V578I, V625F and H608N highest — all buried, all called destabilising by the stability methods, so conservation and physics agree there. It disagrees informatively on two. T108A is the only variant with a negative LOR (−0.51): alanine is more common than threonine at that position across the family.

5. What survives all four methods

  • Seven variants clear +0.5 kcal/mol on all four methods — in descending ThermoMPNN order: I1065T (range 0.82–3.15), Y317H (1.19–2.72), V625F (1.42–5.33), V578I (0.51–1.03), R1063H (0.65–1.04), H608N (0.96–2.11) and F240V (0.68–1.62). Of these only I1065T also clears the ±1.47 kcal/mol per-variant error bar (section 6), so unanimity across methods is necessary but not sufficient for confidence.
  • H380Y is the only variant all four methods score negative (−0.07 / −0.91 / −1.13 / −0.79), but no variant is unanimously stabilising beyond the noise floor: ThermoMPNN's −0.07 sits well inside ±0.5, so three methods carry that call, not four.
  • V617F stays small on every method (0.09–1.05), at the 37th percentile of JAK2's own distribution. The canonical driver mutation is not a destabilising one.
  • E846D remains the clearest disagreement: +1.39 ThermoMPNN against −0.76 FoldX. mCSM (+1.15) and DDMut (+0.29) side with ThermoMPNN, making FoldX the outlier. It sits in the JH2–JH1 linker, outside any annotated domain.
  • mCSM is biased toward destabilising: it labels 22 of 23 variants destabilising, a known characteristic of that tool, so its agreement with others is partly structural.

Median expected r across these 23 is 0.52, close to the benchmark's overall 0.49. C427R sits in the worst category (“involves cysteine”, r = 0.07) — Rosetta, and plausibly any method, should not be trusted on it.

A tempting pattern that does not hold. The three variants in low-reliability categories (C427R, S755R, E846D) are all ones where the four methods split on direction. But across all 23 the relationship is null (Pearson r = −0.03, p = 0.90; Fisher exact on the binary split p = 0.068, n = 3). Treat the category as a flag worth checking, not as a predictor of disagreement.

Only I1065T (+2.32) clearly exceeds the typical error of the method that produced it. The theoretical ceiling from experimental noise is r ≈ 0.70–0.80 (10.1093/bioinformatics/bty880), so a sharper threshold is not available from a better tool.

Not a pathogenicity ranking. ΔΔG is a weak classifier of pathogenicity (best reported AUC ≈ 0.66). Gain-of-function and dominant-negative variants are characteristically mild in |ΔΔG|, which is precisely the pattern here. These numbers describe folding stability only.
