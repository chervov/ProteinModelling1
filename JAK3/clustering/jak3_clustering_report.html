This is the complete feature set, all eleven, including the three dual-state ΔΔG columns. The ΔΔG was not recomputed for this report — it is reused from the overnight PyRosetta run of 22 October 2026 across four molab CPU boxes, documented in data/ddg/RUNBOOK.md and in build_ddg.py here.

Read section 3 before using ΔΔG delta. On JAK2 that feature proved inert (AUC 0.499) and irreproducible between two independent model pairs (r = 0.074). JAK3 has only one pair, so the reproducibility test cannot be repeated here — but the pair is confounded, which is worse, and the same statistical signature is present.

JAK3 has something JAK2 never had: a deep mutational scan. Section 5 validates the clustering against 22,536 experimental measurements rather than 21 ClinVar labels.

1. Method and seeds

Gower distance, which compares each pair of variants only on the features both have, so nothing is imputed. Semi-supervised K-means with 7 seed variants pinned to their clusters. Converged in 10 iterations.

The paper names 10 JAK1 seeds. JAK3 has no published equivalent, so they were derived by an explicit rule from data no feature saw:

  • C1 tolerant — the 5 ClinVar benign/likely-benign variants with the highest gnomAD allele frequency, all 2-star: P132T, V722I, P151R, A1090T, R925S. P1104A is excluded although it ranks 4th by allele frequency: it is ClinVar-benign because it is common, but it is the established JAK3 loss-of-function allele behind the TB-susceptibility literature, so pinning it to the tolerant pole would pin a known functional variant to the wrong cluster.
  • C2 damaging — only two. JAK3 ClinVar holds just two Pathogenic missense records and both are 0-star, where JAK2 had five 2-star ones. Rather than invent three more, the damaging pole is pinned with two seeds and the DMS is kept entirely for validation: D784N, R403C, R582W, R103C, C193Y.
The seeds barely matter. Fifteen random draws of the same sizes reproduce 0.9996–0.9999 of the labels. As on JAK1 and JAK2, the supervision orients the result — which pole is called "Cluster 1" — rather than determining its structure. So the hand-made seed rule above is not load bearing.

3. The dual-state ΔΔG features

Both states were measured, not trusted. 14 of JAK3's 30 selected_*.pdb files are mislabelled, and three OpenFold runs produced compact models that sit 15–16 Å from the autoinhibited arrangement — collapsed into some other state. FERM–JH1 distance alone would have accepted all of them; it took a JH1 RMSD after a JH2 fit to tell them apart.

JAK3 has no experimental closed structure, so the reference is the AlphaFold DB model, which is itself closed. Weaker than TYK2, which has the 4OLI crystal.

The pair is split across two predictors — AF3 closed, OpenFold open — so it carries a provenance confound. It is the mildest of the three: every per-domain RMSD between the two states is 0.31–0.93 Å, against 5.47 Å for TYK2's FERM domain, so these two structures really are related by a motion of JH1 and little else.

ΔΔG delta has now scored chance on every protein tested — JAK1 0.4966, JAK2 0.499, TYK2 0.502. See section 2 for JAK3's value.

4. How the features relate to each other

Every feature is oriented before correlating — ESM-1b, SIFT and BLOSUM62 are multiplied by −1 — so that positive always means "agrees that this is damaging". Without that step the matrix shows large negative correlations that are an artefact of sign convention, not disagreement. After orienting, the lowest correlation in the whole matrix is -0.07: only ddg_delta fails to agree in direction with the rest.

Mean |ρ| across the 28 off-diagonal pairs is 0.42 — these features are not independent evidence. The tightest pairs are CADD/ClinPred (0.85), ddG closed/ddG open (0.85), AlphaMissense/ClinPred (0.81); the loosest are ddG closed/ddG delta (-0.07), BLOSUM62/ddG delta (0.01), ClinPred/ddG delta (0.04).

Two readings matter for the clustering:

  • The redundancy is concentrated. CADD, ClinPred, PolyPhen-2 and SIFT form a block that correlates at 0.75–0.84, which is unsurprising: ClinPred is a meta-predictor trained partly on the others, and all four lean heavily on conservation. Giving each of them weight 1, as the paper's method does, effectively weights that one signal four times. This is the strongest argument for the feature weights the paper does not publish.
  • BLOSUM62 and FoldX are the genuinely independent columns. BLOSUM62 correlates with everything else at 0.24–0.41 and FoldX at 0.39–0.50, against 0.64–0.84 among the rest. They are also the two weakest discriminators in section 2 (AUC 0.667 and 0.740). That is not a contradiction — they contribute information the others lack, which is exactly what a distance over mixed features should use them for, even though neither would be a good classifier alone.

The right-hand panel is the caveat. Pairwise coverage ranges from 6,200 variants — a dbNSFP feature against FoldX, the two incomplete sources intersected — through 6,697 for any two dbNSFP features, up to the full 21,356 among ESM-1b, AlphaMissense, PolyPhen-2 and BLOSUM62. The correlations in the dbNSFP block are therefore computed on a different, smaller and non-random subset than the ones among the complete features — the single-nucleotide-reachable 31.1% — so they are not strictly comparable to each other. The same asymmetry is what makes the gnomAD stratification in section 5 necessary.

5. gnomAD — the external test

gnomAD r4 holds 1,343 of these substitutions as observed human variation. No feature saw it.

The damaging cluster is depleted of observed variation — C1 8.05% against C2 4.89%, Fisher p = 5.0e-21. But the pooled odds ratio of 1.7 is confounded, exactly as on JAK1: 99.9% of gnomAD-matched variants fall in the dbNSFP-reachable subset, which is only 30.9% of all variants, and the tolerant cluster is disproportionately inside it (33.7% against 28.8%). Stratifying gives Mantel–Haenszel OR 1.53, which is the figure to quote.

9. What this is, and what it is not

  • Not a reproduction. There is no published JAK3 clustering. Nothing here is an ARI against anyone.
  • All eleven features, but the conformational axis is inert — see section 3.
  • FoldX stands in for "MutFunc", as on JAK1, and is 94.6% rather than complete — the missing positions are 1–38, which ProtVar trims as AlphaFold's low-confidence N-terminus.
  • PolyPhen-2 HumVar was used. The paper does not name a model, and HumDiv disagrees on 23.4% of JAK3 variants. That choice is unexamined here and mattered more than any other on JAK1.
  • Equal feature weights — the paper's are unpublished. With ddg_closed and ddg_open correlating at 0.896 they are effectively one feature given double weight here.
  • ΔΔG from a single conformer per state, not an ensemble, and the interface residues where a conformational difference could be real sit in the least confident part of the model (AlphaFold FERM–JH1 PAE 21.0 Å against a 31 Å ceiling).
  • The gnomAD test is not mutability-controlled. CpG sites mutate ~10× faster.
